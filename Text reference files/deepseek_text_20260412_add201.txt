h2. The Challenge

A Permian Basin operator was experiencing frequent ESP failures across a 12-well pad. Production downtime averaged 8 days per month per well, with replacement costs exceeding $150,000 per failure.

Key pain points identified:
- Rapid gas interference causing pump-off conditions
- Inconsistent fluid properties due to changing GOR
- No real-time monitoring or automated control
- Reactive maintenance instead of predictive

h2. Our Approach

TerraQuint deployed a three-phase solution.

h3. Phase 1: Data Diagnostics

We installed real-time sensors and collected 90 days of operational data including pump intake pressure, motor amperage, discharge pressure, fluid temperature, and vibration data.

h3. Phase 2: Nodal Analysis Integration

Using Petroleum Experts IPM and WellFlo, we built a complete system model from reservoir to sales. The model identified optimal operating frequency range of 52-58 Hz, critical minimum intake pressure of 280 psi, and gas interference threshold of 15% free gas at pump intake.

h3. Phase 3: Real-time Control Implementation

We implemented an automated control system that adjusts VFD frequency based on intake pressure, triggers alarms when parameters deviate, and provides daily optimization recommendations.

h2. The Results

After 6 months of operation:

Production Uptime: 73% before, 96% after = +23% improvement
ESP Failure Rate: 4 per year before, 1 per year after = -75% improvement
Average Run Life: 214 days before, 412 days after = +93% improvement
Operating Cost: $42,000/month before, $31,000/month after = -26% improvement

h2. Key Takeaways

1. Real-time data is essential for ESP optimization
2. Nodal analysis helps identify root causes
3. Automated control pays for itself within 3-4 months
4. Regular monitoring prevents catastrophic failures

h2. Conclusion

This case study demonstrates that a data-driven approach to ESP optimization delivers substantial improvements in uptime, reliability, and operating costs.
